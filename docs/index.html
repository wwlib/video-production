01The song: “Code of Life” becomes “Code de Vie”

It started as an original English song about developers who build software for healthcare. It was produced in Suno as “Code of Life”, then turned into a French/English version, which became the film's soundtrack.

Original English song: “Code of Life”
Final French master (used in the video): “Code de Vie”

Iterations. The first lyrics file is dated 24 Oct, 12:09. Song takes followed the same day (“code-of-life” takes 1–3, then a v3 set), and a v4 set on 25 Oct. The French/English master code-de-vie.wav landed on 25 Oct at 11:56.
A spliced verse 2. The v3 master takes a 30 s verse-2 passage (75.5–105.5 s) from another take. It is joined with a 35 ms crossfade and its loudness is matched, so the song never had to be regenerated.
The real tempo. Beat tracking measured 129.9 bpm (0.4617 s per beat), not the nominal value. Bar downbeats sit on the section starts; chord changes land on that beat about twice as often as on the other beats. That grid later drives the cuts, the light leaks and the EKG.
Lyrics alignment. A forced alignment gives word-level timings for all 27 lines. They feed the lip-sync audio windows, the subtitles and, at the very end, the timing of the “Code de Vie” title.

02The pipeline

Everything was rendered locally on one consumer GPU, and all post work happened on a separate Linux box. The binding constraint turned out to be system RAM, not VRAM.

GPU box. ComfyUI portable 0.42.1, PyTorch 2.14 + cu130, RTX 4070 SUPER 12 GB, 16 GB system RAM. The readiness check found no driver or ComfyUI update was needed. It also showed that RAM is the limit.
Stills. Qwen-Image-2.1 Turbo (int8) with a Qwen3-VL text encoder. Every shot starts from a generated first frame.
Motion. Wan 2.2 I2V A14B (Q4 GGUF, high- and low-noise experts) with the lightx2v 4-step LoRA, 81 frames at 16 fps and 832×480, then retimed to 24 fps with film grain.
Lip sync. ComfyUI's built-in InfiniteTalk on the Wan 480p base, driven by the separated vocal stem. Long lines later became one continuous take using native windowing (windows of at most 81 frames, 9 motion frames carried over).
Post. ffmpeg + Python/OpenCV on the box: cut builds, the vscode_comp screen compositor, YuNet face checks, rembg person mattes, subtitles (ASS), FX and audio.
Considered, not used. MiniMax H3 was ruled out: its licence excludes local use in the US/EU/UK/Korea and it needs at least 32 GB RAM. The Wan Animate pose-driven route was tested in pose/ but not adopted.

03The Claudia era

The first pass used Claudia, an open character by anabology. It established the story, the rules for the engineer, the screen compositing and the v3 full cut that set the bar.

// key decisions

  • A dual story. An engineer at her monitors and a nurse at the bedside, cut together on the beat as two worlds.
  • Engineer rules. She always looks at her screen, never the lens. No rain on the glass. Her monitors are rendered blank, and real VS Code screens are composited in post.
  • Thin dark glasses for the engineer only. They separate the two roles at a glance. The nurse never wears them.
  • Reworking the intro. Seven intro rough cuts (25 Oct 19:23 → 22:53). Among other changes, an intro shot was replaced by a hand-on-mouse shot.
  • The v3 full cut. All 73 shots are real clips with lip sync, VS Code composites, HUD cards and English subtitles. Built 26 Oct 14:54, and loved by the director.

04An original character: Jen

To own the film outright, the team designed an original lead. The face went through text-only iterations. The director's reference photos were used only as written direction, never as an image or likeness input.

Canon (locked 28 Oct, 23:29). An adult woman in her mid-20s with a walnut-brown high ponytail, fair skin, light blue-grey eyes, a softly rounded face and minimal makeup.
Safety by design. She always clearly reads as an adult and is never sexualised. Takes that read under about 22 are rejected. The reference sheet outfit is a plain white tank top and leggings, and the canon avoids glossy lips, doll or anime proportions, and suggestive poses.
Two roles. The engineer wears a grey hoodie and thin dark glasses, with her eyes on the screen. The nurse wears periwinkle scrubs (changed from teal on 30 Oct), no glasses, in a bright daylight hospital.

05Jen v4: the engineer who imagines herself as a nurse

The story was reframed: Jen writes software for nurses and imagines herself in their shoes. The director's 30 Oct shot notes set the tone: bright, cheerful, periwinkle scrubs, and no dancing, only bobbing and tapping to the beat. Shot 18 is the single moment she looks into the camera.

06Notes & fix rounds

The director reviewed every cut and sent shot-by-shot notes. Each round was batched and kept frugal: one take per fix, a retry only when something was clearly broken, and short consolidated reports.

Realistic skin. Prompts added visible pores and slight unevenness, and the negatives added “plastic skin, airbrushed, waxy”.
Keyed-out bodies. In shots 11 and 65 the screen composite showed through her hood. A person-segmentation matte (rembg) now holds her in front of the screens.
Lip-sync seams. Shots 9, 18 and 31 had visible joins. They were re-rendered as single continuous InfiniteTalk takes, and frame-difference checks found no jumps.
Casting the nurses. Crowd shots shouldn't all be Jen. Shot 49 went through two fixes and ended with one nurse Jen and one different nurse.
Subtitles on every line. First French lines only, then English on the English-sung lines too: 27 cues, kept off faces by automatic face checks.
Honest reporting. Each fix is listed, and flaws are flagged rather than hidden.

07FX: two worlds, one pulse

A post-only look pass (fx/apply_fx.py + config.json) was tested on 20–25 s excerpts before any full run.

  • techCool teal/blue grade, a slight chromatic fringe at the edges, faint scanlines and monitor glow, and code drifting at the frame edges.
  • hospitalWarm sunny grade with bloom and halation, small vitals and checkmark callouts, and whites and blacks protected so it doesn't go milky.
  • Glitch cut back. v1 glitched every world switch. The director found it distracting, so it now fires only at 5 key crossings. A person matte keeps the glitch blocks off Jen.
  • Grain removed. The 16 mm grain read as noise, so it was dropped.
  • An EKG pulse. A bright white line with a dark shadow, locked to the real ~130 bpm grid, with a much taller spike on beat 1 of every bar.
  • “Code de Vie” title. Typed in DejaVu Sans Mono with a blinking cursor at every sung “Code de vie” (6 moments). A JavaScript DNA-helix code stream falls behind it, ending in a glitch-tear on the title only.

07bFX round 2: distress, a future hospital and holograms (v5 → v6)

A second look pass split the two worlds further. Every idea was first tested on a 15–20 s excerpt with a contact sheet, then rolled across the film in three sections and finally rendered in one pass.

  • coderTech distress. Modeled on a reference clip: clean footage, with the damage living in the screens. The tracked monitor and VS Code regions get scanlines, jitter and slipping bands. Hard hits of slice-tearing, RGB split and macroblocks are locked to the beat grid (big on beat 1 of every bar, smaller on beats 3 and 4). Red “✗ tests failed” flashes tint a single screen. v1 was sparing, v2 went loud “right off the bat”, and v6 backs it off about 25%: fewer small hits and flashes and gentler tears, with every bar-1 hit kept. A person matte keeps the damage off Jen, apart from one-frame full-frame flashes.
  • nurseFuturistic hyper-real grade. A 60s Kodachrome look and a futuristic look were compared side by side, and the futuristic one won: cool, clean whites, a slight teal cast in the shadows, midtone clarity, gentle sharpening (eased to avoid halos), a soft highlight glow and a dreamy bloom on the windows. An “angelic” halo glows around Jen's silhouette, never over her face. No grain.
  • Hologram meshes. Sparse wireframes with fine 1 px glowing lines appear on distinct shapes only: the hallway, the ward beds, a vitals monitor and a patient's blanket (dark-teal lines over a slight darkening so they read on white). They fade in on bar-1 downbeats with a scan line sweeping through. Jen gets an occasional light whole-body wireframe built from her silhouette outline plus a dozen pose joints. A dense face-scan mesh with glowing eyes was tried and rejected because it hid her face.
  • How it's built. Python with NumPy and OpenCV does every frame; ffmpeg handles decoding, encoding, subtitles and the audio mux. rembg (u2net human segmentation) gives the person mattes, a YuNet detector keeps effects off faces, and mediapipe provides the face and pose landmarks. Tracked screen corners from the VS Code composites place the screen effects, and a beat grid (about 129.9 bpm, bar phase verified on the chords and snare) times every hit, flash and mesh.

08The ending: letting “Code de vie” breathe

The last sung “Code de vie” was cut off abruptly in the generated song. Rather than going back to Suno, a reverb was applied to the separated vocal stem only. The voice blooms and decays to silence over about 2.5 s with no click. The picture holds the last frame and fades to black under a Team XRay credit.
